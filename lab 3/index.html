What Is

What is domestic violence and gender-based violence?

Domestic violence is abuse by someone in your family or household. GBV is harm aimed at a person because of their gender. Both can happen to anyone, and both go beyond physical injury.

Physical

Hitting, pushing, choking, burning, or blocking you from leaving a room or the house.

Emotional and psychological

Constant insults, threats, humiliation, jealousy, or making you doubt your own memory and worth.

Economic

Taking your income, forbidding you to work, hiding money, or refusing to pay for food and basic needs.

Sexual

Any sexual act without your consent, including within a marriage or relationship.

Digital

Tracking your phone, reading your messages, sharing private images, or harassing you online.

Gender-based

Harassment, stalking, or control linked to someone's gender, at home, at work, in public, or online.

Signs that a relationship may be abusive

  • You feel afraid of how they will react.
  • They control who you see, where you go, or what you spend.
  • They blame you for their anger or hurt you and then apologise.
  • They threaten you, your children, or your pets.
  • You hide bruises or make excuses for them.

Common myths

Myth: It is a private family matter.
It is a crime, and the law gives you ways to be protected.
Myth: If it was that bad, they would just leave.
Leaving can be the most dangerous time. Money, children, and fear all play a part.
Myth: Only women are victims.
The 2017 amendment to the Domestic Violence Act protects family members of any gender.

Law Enforcement

What the law says

Malaysian law treats domestic violence and GBV as crimes. These are the main Acts you will hear about.

Domestic Violence Act 1994Act 521, amended 2017

Lets victims get protection orders. After the 2017 amendment it covers psychological abuse, harassment by any medium such as text messages, and all family members regardless of gender. Breaching a protection order is an offence, with a fine and possible jail time.

Penal CodeAct 574

Covers assault, causing hurt, criminal intimidation, rape, and other sexual offences. Police can investigate these whether or not a protection order exists.

Child Act 2001Act 611

Protects anyone under 18 from abuse, neglect, and abandonment, and sets out how children in need of protection are cared for.

Sexual Offences Against Children Act 2017Act 792

Creates specific offences for sexual crimes against children and sets out how child witnesses are supported in court.

Anti-Sexual Harassment Act 2022Act 840

Set up the Tribunal for Anti-Sexual Harassment, where a person can seek a remedy for sexual harassment without going through a full criminal case.

Getting a protection order

  1. Get to safety and get careGo to a trusted place. If you are hurt, visit a government hospital. Many have a One-Stop Crisis Centre.
  2. Make a police reportYou can do this at any police station. Ask for a copy of your report.
  3. Ask for an Emergency Protection OrderA social welfare officer can issue one, including on weekends. It lasts 7 days and needs no court application.
  4. Apply to the courtThe court can grant an interim protection order while your case is heard, then a longer protection order.
  5. Report any breachIf the order is ignored, tell the police straight away.

Good to know

  • Keep screenshots, medical reports, photos, and messages somewhere the abuser cannot reach.
  • You can ask for legal help from the Legal Aid Department or an NGO such as AWAM or WAO.
  • Family matters for Muslims can also involve the Syariah courts. An NGO can explain which route fits your case.
  • This page is general information, not legal advice. Check the Acts or speak to a lawyer for your situation.

Enforcer

Who acts when you ask for help

Several agencies share the work of protecting victims and holding abusers to account.

Royal Malaysia Police (PDRM)

Takes your report and investigates. The D11 division handles sexual, women, and child investigations, and many districts have dedicated officers.

Department of Social Welfare (JKM)

Protection officers can issue Emergency Protection Orders, arrange shelter, and support children at risk.

Government hospitals

Treat injuries, document them for evidence, and refer you on. One-Stop Crisis Centres bring medical, police, and welfare help together.

The courts

Grant protection orders and hear criminal cases. Syariah courts handle certain family matters for Muslims.

Legal Aid Department

Gives legal advice and, for those who qualify, help with representation in court.

NGOs and shelters

WAO, AWAM, WCC Penang and others offer counselling, legal information, safe housing, and someone to go with you to the police or court.
